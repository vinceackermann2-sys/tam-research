# TAM v3 100M / 2B Verdict — 2026-08-22

Status: evidence ledger. This records observed results and does not retroactively change preregistered criteria.

## Matched training setup

TAM-v3 and the ordinary Transformer were matched on:

- ~101.8M parameters
- exact same 2B pretraining token bytes
- exact same 5M validation bytes
- GPT-2 tokenizer
- context 512
- seed 8100
- microbatch 64
- gradient accumulation 2
- AdamW betas 0.9/0.95
- weight decay 0.1
- LR 3e-4 with 2% warmup/cosine schedule
- same H100 class
- same SFT arrays and hyperparameters
- same DPO arrays and hyperparameters

Parameter counts:

- TAM-v3: 101,806,616
- Transformer: 101,803,520
- difference: 3,096 parameters (~0.003%)

## Training results

| Metric | TAM-v3 | Transformer | Better |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| pretrain NLL | 2.6984 | 2.7116 | TAM |
| pretrain PPL | 14.86 | 15.05 | TAM |
| SFT assistant NLL | 1.7839 | 1.7947 | TAM |
| DPO held-out reward accuracy | 62.0% | 62.6% | Transformer |
| final mixture NLL | 2.8439 | 2.8647 | TAM |
| steady train throughput | ~254.8k tok/s | ~319.9k tok/s | Transformer |
| full H100 pipeline | ~9,195.5s | ~6,916.8s | Transformer |

Interpretation: TAM-v3 retains a small equal-token language-modeling advantage but pays a large hardware-efficiency penalty.

## Matched synthetic state/memory benchmark

Four tasks (delayed recall, associative recall, state tracking, needle retrieval), seven tested lengths, 96 paired trials per task/length.

Length deltas (TAM - Transformer):

- 128: -2.08 pp, 95% CI -7.03 to +2.60
- 256: +7.81 pp, 95% CI +2.60 to +13.54
- 384: +2.86 pp, 95% CI -2.60 to +8.33
- 512: -0.52 pp, 95% CI -5.73 to +4.69
- 640: -1.04 pp, 95% CI -6.51 to +4.69
- 768: -0.78 pp, 95% CI -6.25 to +4.69
- 1000: +1.04 pp, 95% CI -4.43 to +6.51

Aggregates:

- <=512: TAM +2.02 pp, 95% CI -0.72 to +4.69
- >512 extrapolation: TAM -0.26 pp, 95% CI -3.39 to +3.04
- overall: TAM +1.04 pp, 95% CI -1.00 to +3.05

Pooled task accuracy across all lengths:

- delayed recall: TAM 33.18% vs Transformer 32.59%
- associative recall: TAM 26.34% vs Transformer 24.70%
- state tracking: TAM 24.70% vs Transformer 25.00%
- needle retrieval: TAM 24.26% vs Transformer 22.02%

At 256 tokens, the strongest signals were:

- state tracking: +11.5 pp
- needle retrieval: +12.5 pp (paired 95% CI +2.1 to +22.9)

The learned TAM-v3 mixer averaged ~44.69% attention / ~55.31% world-state contribution, so the recurrent branch was not ignored.

## Final verdict

TAM-v3 is **not a breakthrough and not currently a superior general-purpose replacement for the Transformer**.

Evidence in favor:

- lower matched equal-token NLL/PPL
- repeated smaller-scale loss wins in earlier 25M/50M work
- a targeted state/needle signal at 256 tokens
- learned world-state utilization is substantial

Evidence against immediate scale-up:

- ordinary Transformer is materially faster
- Transformer wins most standard downstream benchmarks
- no broad >512-context/state advantage
- only one fully matched 100M/2B seed

Decision:

1. Do not blindly scale TAM-v3 to 300M/1B.
2. Build/test TAM-v4 at small scale first.
3. TAM-v4 must directly address persistent cross-chunk state and conditional compute.
4. In parallel, explore a clean-sheet architecture that targets conditional computation, adaptive depth, memory hierarchy, continual/local learning, latent reasoning, parallel generation, and multimodality.
