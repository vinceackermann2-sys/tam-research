## Research question and prior failure
V12 original hybrid and matched Transformer improved on known stationary/single-flip synthetic sensor worlds using a hand-specified Bayesian belief, but on **unseen two sensor flips** the same feature reduced hybrid reward by **0.0852** and Transformer reward by **0.1233** relative to raw. The original hybrid did not beat the Transformer on familiar tasks. Does a causal probability of being outside the **known hypothesis set** protect the neural system from incorrect beliefs, relative to simple raw-only, belief-only and random-gating controls?

From 6 **observed** target/reference calibration pairs and known signal strengths, enumerate **all 32 target-sensor sign histories** relative to final sensor sign (the final calibration observation matches query polarity). Known four patterns: stationary and one sensor sign flip at time 2,3,4; prior among known is 0.5 stationary and 0.5/3 each one-flip. The other 28 sign histories are an explicitly **model-specified alternative hypothesis set**, uniform conditional prior. Group priors P(known)=P(alternative)=0.5. Integrate all hidden calibration bits analytically. Compute P(alternative | observed calibration), **never** reading private true polarity, family label, hidden switch time, query truth, or any unpurchased probe. **Frozen fallback threshold = 0.5**: choose the separately trained raw model when alternative posterior > 0.5, else choose a separately trained original-v12 belief-augmented model. The selection happens before probe purchase; the selected model chooses the information action and final action. The fallback carries **TWO full 64k neural policies** and extra likelihood calculations; parameter count/CPU burden is not parity matched to a single control.

## Immutable advancement criteria
**Gate A, robust fallback usefulness (further CPU only), ALL conditions for at least one of the two full backbones:** double-flip fallback mean reward must improve >= **0.04** versus always-belief, be within **0.015** of always-raw, and beat always-belief in >=2/3 paired seeds. In both familiar **base** and **one_flip**, fallback may lose no more than **0.015** vs always-belief. In double_flip it must beat the *shuffled fallback assignment control* by >= **0.015**, ensuring evidence is useful. Detector must fall back >0.60 on double_flip but <0.40 on stationary. **reference_drift** fallback may not be >0.02 worse than always-raw. If ANY fail, flag no-go without rounding or cherry-picking.
**Gate B, original architecture advantage (separate):** hybrid fallback beats matched Transformer fallback by >=0.015 mean net reward in BOTH stationary and one_flip with >=2/3 seed wins, does not lose two_flip OR reference_drift by >0.015, and <=1.5× Transformer actual CPU training time. Two backbones serving one decision is more expensive than either standalone network; explicitly report that and reject any FLOP/efficiency claim.
**Gate C, genuinely unknown-world transfer:** alternative class enumerates double_flip histories. For reference-drift, outside *both* models, negative or weak detection is a failure of generalized novelty recognition even if gate A partially passes. An expanded Bayesian likelihood is prior knowledge, not new SI theory. Stop if no benefit; no GPU authorization, breakthrough, scaling, automation or main merge even on a win.
